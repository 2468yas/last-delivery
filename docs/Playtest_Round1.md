## 1. Test Plan

### Area being tested

Testers play the first delivery of the Monday shift in our Figma prototype. It covers the whole core loop once: picking up the order at Corner Slice, choosing a route, reacting to a hazard, keeping the pizza stack steady, reading the clue at the house, knocking, reading the resident's note, and seeing the Order Complete screen with tips and memories.

### Player actions and objective

**Objective we give the tester:** "Deliver the Large Veggie pizza to 14 Elm St." We don't explain anything else, so we can see if the prototype teaches itself.

**Actions the tester can take:**

- Press Pick Up at Corner Slice.
- Choose a route: Bell Hill (short but steep) or Downtown (long but flat).
- On Downtown, react to a car: Slow Down or Run Past.
- When the stack wobbles, press Steady within 1.5 seconds.
- At the house, read the clue and press Knock.
- Read June's note, press Continue, then review the Order Complete screen.

**Possible outcomes:** the pizza arrives intact (house gets brighter, warm note, +$12 tips) or crushed (house fades, a shadow figure appears, bitter note, +$2 tips). After the first run, we ask the tester to play once more and try something different, so they see both outcomes.

### Requested feedback

We want to know if players understand the goal, the difference between the two routes, how much time they have on the Steady check, whether they notice the clue at the door, and whether the notes and memory fragments make them curious about the story. These are covered by the post-play survey below.

### Involuntary feedback (what we watch for)

- Which route they pick first, and how long they take to decide.
- Whether they miss the 1.5 second Steady window. If most testers miss it, the timing or the warning may need to change.
- Whether they notice the "Veggie garden = no meat" clue, or skip past it.
- Whether they read June's note or click Continue right away.
- Whether they notice the house getting brighter or fading, and the shadow figure.
- Misclicks, hesitation, or clicking on things that are not buttons.
- Any comments they say out loud while playing.

### Rounds and time

Each tester does 2 runs of the delivery (about 2 to 3 minutes each), then fills out the survey (about 5 minutes). That is about 10 minutes per tester and about 1 hour for all 6 testers.

### Script we read to each tester

"This is a prototype, so some parts are simple. Your goal is to deliver the Large Veggie pizza to 14 Elm St. Please think out loud while you play. We won't help during the test, but we'll answer questions after."

## 2. Post-Play Survey

Likert questions use a scale from 1 to 5.

1. How clear was the goal of the delivery? (1 = not clear at all, 5 = very clear)
2. How clear was the difference between the Bell Hill and Downtown routes? (1 = not clear at all, 5 = very clear)
3. Which route did you take first, and why did you pick it? (open-ended)
4. How much time did you feel you had to press Steady? (1 = far too little, 3 = about right, 5 = far too much)
5. How noticeable was the clue at the door (the veggie garden)? (1 = didn't notice it, 5 = very noticeable)
6. How well did you understand what June's note was telling you? (1 = not at all, 5 = completely)
7. How clear was what the Memories counter tracks? (1 = not clear at all, 5 = very clear)
8. How easy was the top bar (deliveries, memories, address, time, money) to read? (1 = very hard to read, 5 = very easy to read)
9. After the order was complete, how much did you want to see the next order? (1 = not at all, 5 = very much)
10. In your own words, what do you think happens when a pizza gets crushed? (open-ended)
11. What do you think the story of the game is about so far? (open-ended)
12. Was there any moment where you weren't sure what to do? If so, when? (open-ended)
13. If you could change one thing in this prototype, what would it be? (open-ended)
